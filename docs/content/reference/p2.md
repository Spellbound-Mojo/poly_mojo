# Division, gcds, and resultants

The library implements division, gcds, resultants, and canonical rational functions.
Every computational entry point accepts the caller's shared `Budget`.
Coefficients support `ZZ`, `QQ`, and prime `ModInt`; rational functions use `QQ`.

## Representation and boundary conventions

- Exact sparse division accepts KERNEL storage order, uses an internal LEX
  order for termination, and restores the original order. Ordered list
  division requires LEX, GRLEX, or GREVLEX.
- `subresultants` returns the entire indexed family, including zero entries.
  Resultants use the Sylvester sign convention in E-1. A zero input gives
  zero; one nonzero constant gives the usual power. Two nonzero constants
  in the elimination variable raise `DOMAIN`: E-1 does not define this
  case, and the empty determinant 1 need not belong to their ideal over ZZ.
- `check_resultant_identity` proves ideal membership; it does not prove that
  the supplied polynomial is the determinant. `check_common_divisor` proves
  divisibility; maximality additionally needs a Bézout or coprimality proof.
- The gcd of two zeros is zero, with zero cofactors. A requested field proof
  can use the zero Bézout identity; no coprimality proof exists for two zero
  cofactors over ZZ.
- Integer and rational coprimality certificates can carry a degree-preserving
  modular affine projection and a univariate Bézout identity. Integer witnesses
  also exclude scalar factors through an exact coefficient identity.
  The fallback coprimality nodes carry explicit content witnesses.
  Node references point backwards. A unit identity terminates a proof;
  other nodes prove the contents through coefficient gcd folds. The checker
  visits every referenced input pair, so sharing a node cannot substitute
  a proof of a different pair. It does not run gcd, division, or resultants.
- Cancellation certificates establish the original guarded equality, including
  denominator scalar normalization. They do not assert equality at removed
  singularities or prove maximal cancellation on their own.
